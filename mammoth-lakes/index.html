Mammoth Lakes

Alpine lakes, granite peaks, and a basin full of campgrounds within a few minutes of each other.

A mix of short lake walks and the bigger days out toward Duck Pass and Devils Postpile.

Things to do

  • Take the Panorama Gondola up Mammoth Mountain for a 360-degree view of the Sierra, even in summer
  • Soak at Wild Willy's or one of the other undeveloped hot springs out on the Long Valley side roads
  • Fish Lake Mary, Lake Mamie, or the creeks in the basin; a California license is required
  • Ride the free trolley around the Lakes Basin instead of driving and hunting for parking
  • Drive up to Minaret Vista at sunset for the best view of the Ritter Range

Know before you go

Most campgrounds in the Lakes Basin sit between 8,600 and 9,000 feet, so expect cold nights year-round and afternoon thunderstorms most summer afternoons. The campground season generally runs June through September; snow can close roads into the basin well into June some years. Devils Postpile and the Rainbow Falls trailhead require the mandatory shuttle bus from Mammoth Mountain Adventure Center during the day in summer, with your own car only allowed early morning or evening.

Black bears are active throughout the area, and bear boxes are standard at every campground. The altitude catches people out more than the mileage does; give yourself a day to acclimate before a big hike like Duck Pass. Cell service is spotty once you're in the basin itself, so plan meeting points and download maps ahead of time.
