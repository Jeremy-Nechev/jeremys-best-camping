Tulare County

Mountain Home State Forest

A working demonstration forest above Springville, with its own grove of giant sequoias and a handful of first-come campgrounds. There's no store, no gas, and barely any cell service once you're in, so bring what you need.

Where to camp

Shake Camp, Hedrick Pond, Moses Gulch, Sunset Point & Balch Park

Five small first-come campgrounds within a few miles of each other, split between the state forest and the adjoining Balch Park county park next door. Close enough to treat as one cluster, if one's full, the next is only a short drive away.

Elevation
5,400–6,950 ft
Sites
71 total
Season
May–Oct
Reserve
First-come
  • Shake Camp: entrance-area, near the equestrian staging area and the old fire lookout, a good base for the whole forest
  • Hedrick Pond: smallest and shadiest, right next to a stocked fishing pond
  • Moses Gulch: lowest and warmest, creekside near an old schoolhouse site
  • Sunset Point: highest and most scenic, on a ridge with a real view, but tiny and quick to fill
  • Balch Park: county-run and just past the forest boundary, with its own sequoia grove and the largest campground of the five

Things to do

  • Fish or picnic at Hedrick Pond
  • Ride or pack in from the Shake Camp equestrian staging area, popular with horse packers heading into the adjacent wilderness
  • Stargaze from Sunset Point; there's no light pollution for miles
  • Walk the grove at Balch Park and cool off at its small swimming spot
  • Cool off at a swimming hole on the Tule River near Camp Nelson, on the drive up

Know before you go

There is no store, restaurant, or gas station inside Mountain Home. Stock up before you turn off the highway, either in Springville, about 45 minutes down the mountain via Bear Creek and Balch Park Road, or at the small store and lodge in Ponderosa, about 30 minutes away on Highway 190. Cell service inside the forest is unreliable to nonexistent, don't count on it.

Bear Creek Road and Balch Park Road are steep, narrow, and switchback heavily in places; they're not recommended for long trailers or large RVs, and both close with the first significant snow, usually by November. This is a working demonstration forest managed by CAL FIRE, so expect the occasional logging truck or active harvest area, and stay out of any signed closure.

Some campgrounds have potable water spigots, but not all, and not every year, bring backup water regardless of which one you book. Campfire and stove restrictions are common in late summer, check current conditions before you go.
