Where to camp

Shake Camp, Hedrick Pond, Moses Gulch, Sunset Point & Balch Park

Five small first-come campgrounds within a few miles of each other, split between the state forest and the adjoining Balch Park county park. If one is full, the next is only a short drive away.

Elevation
5,400–6,950 ft
Sites
71 total
Season
May–Oct
Reserve
First-come
  • Shake Camp: entrance-area, near the equestrian staging area and the old fire lookout, a good base for the whole forest
  • Hedrick Pond: smallest and shadiest, right next to a stocked fishing pond
  • Moses Gulch: lowest and warmest, creekside near an old schoolhouse site
  • Sunset Point: highest and most scenic, on a ridge with a real view, but tiny and quick to fill
  • Balch Park: county-run and just past the forest boundary, with its own sequoia grove. It is the largest campground of the five

Things to do

  • Fish or picnic at Hedrick Pond
  • Look at the great view from Sunset Point
  • Walk the grove at Balch Park and cool off at its small swimming spot

Know before you go

There is no store, restaurant, or gas station inside Mountain Home, so make sure you bring everything you need. The closest store is in Springville, about 45 minutes down the mountain. Cell service inside the forest is very bad.

Bear Creek Road and Balch Park Road are steep, narrow, and switchback heavily in places so know this ahead of time and dont bring long trailers or large RVs.

Some campgrounds have potable water spigots, but not all, so it is good to bring backup water. Along the road on the way to the campground, there is a small spring where you can fill up.
