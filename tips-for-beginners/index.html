Your first trip

Camping is easy to enjoy and easy to get wrong on the first try. These six things fix most of what goes wrong.

  1. 01

    Pick somewhere close

    Your first trip should be under two hours from home and have flush toilets. Save the remote stuff for trip three.

  2. 02

    Book a real site

    Reserved beats first-come when you're learning. Arriving at 6 p.m. to a full campground is how people decide they hate camping.

  3. 03

    Set the tent up in the yard

    Once, before you go. Find the missing pole at home rather than in the dark.

  4. 04

    Plan easy food

    Two dinners you've cooked before. Camp is a bad place to try a new recipe.

  5. 05

    Arrive with daylight left

    Give yourself two hours before sunset. Everything is harder with a headlamp.

  6. 06

    Bring more warm layers than you think

    The temperature drop after dark is the thing first-timers underestimate most.

Common questions

Is camping with young kids actually worth it? Yes, but pick the trip for their attention span, not yours. A drive-up campground with flush toilets and a short, flat trail nearby will go better than a remote site with a long hike, and it's easier to build confidence from an easy first trip than to recover from a hard one.

How cold does it actually get at night? Colder than the daytime forecast suggests, especially above 5,000 feet. Expect a 30 to 40 degree drop after sunset even in summer, and pack layers and a sleeping bag rated below what you think you'll need.

Do I need a bear canister or bear box? If the campground provides a metal food locker, use it every night, even for scented items like toothpaste and sunscreen. It's required in bear country, and rangers do check.

What if it's fully booked? Cancellations post regularly on recreation.gov, and checking a few times a week, especially close to your date, often turns up an opening. First-come, first-served campgrounds are also worth keeping as a backup.

Do I need a permit for anything? Some trails, especially into wilderness areas like San Jacinto or Half Dome, require a separate permit beyond your campsite reservation. Check the specific park or trail page before you go.
