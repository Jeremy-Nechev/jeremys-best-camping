How to choose

Lake Tahoe is the big-lake trip: granite shoreline, cold clear water, and enough campgrounds around the basin to base out of for a week. It's the pick if you want a real lake and a town nearby for a night off from cooking.

Mammoth Lakes is alpine basin camping at altitude, with more lakes within a few minutes of each other than anywhere else on this list. Mono Lake & June Lake sits right next door and shares the same drive, but trades a couple of the highest peaks for tufa towers and a quieter lake loop, a good add-on or a calmer alternative if Mammoth is booked out.

Big Sur is the odd one out geographically, redwood canyons on one side of Highway 1 and cold Pacific surf on the other, but it shares the same booking rhythm as the Sierra sites here: reserve the day the window opens or you won't get in.

Trail of 100 Giants & Ponderosa and Mountain Home State Forest are the odd ones out in a different way, giant sequoias in the Southern Sierra foothills above Springville, closer to Los Angeles than any of the other five destinations here. Almost everything is first-come rather than reserved, so they're a good fallback when Lake Tahoe or Mammoth Lakes are booked solid.

Lake Tahoe, Mammoth Lakes, Mono Lake & June Lake, and Big Sur all run on the same 6 month reservation window, so treat those four as a single list to check on release day. Trail of 100 Giants and Mountain Home State Forest don't need that kind of planning, just an early arrival.
