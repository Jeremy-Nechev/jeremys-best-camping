San Jacinto Mountains

Idyllwild

Boulders, Jeffrey pine, and a walkable town at the center of it all.

Where to camp

Idyllwild County Park

Close to the town, but it still feels secluded. Shaded sites with a variety of hilly and creek areas.

5,400 ft

Year-round

High and remote at 7,300 feet, up a rough dirt road. The views pay for the drive.

Hikes

A few short, flat options for families with little ones, plus the classics if you're up for more.

A gentle, mostly flat path contouring above town through Jeffrey pine and cedar.

Things to do

  • Walk the village end to end for restaurants and bookstores that punch above the town's size; plan an evening off from camp cooking
  • Rock climb the granite at Tahquitz or Suicide Rock, or book a lesson with a local outfitter
  • Photograph Lily Rock from town, even if you don't climb it
  • Ride the Palm Springs Aerial Tramway, an easy detour down the mountain, for trails into the same wilderness from the desert side
  • Pick up a Junior Ranger-style activity booklet at the county park's nature center
  • Let kids burn energy at the village playground if anyone needs a break from the trail

Know before you go

Idyllwild sits at about 5,400 feet, so summer days are mild and nights are genuinely cold; a good sleeping bag matters more here than the daytime forecast suggests. Winter can bring snow that closes Highway 243, the main route up from the desert side, so check road conditions before a cold-season trip.

Any hike into the San Jacinto Wilderness, including the trail to Saddle Junction, requires a free wilderness permit even for a day trip; get it in advance online or at the ranger station in town, since daily quotas do fill. A National Forest Adventure Pass is required to park at most trailheads outside the campgrounds themselves.
