Mountains and desert

San Diego Area

Oak-shaded mountains and open desert, all within about two hours of the city, with a cabin option if you'd rather skip the tent.

Where to camp

Mountain campgrounds under oak and pine, plus two desert options down in Anza-Borrego if you want warm winter sun instead.

Things to do

This region covers more ground than its name suggests: high pine country at Laguna, sequoia-scale conifers at Palomar, oak woodland around Julian, and open desert down in Anza-Borrego, all within about two hours of the city.

  • Tour the Palomar Observatory, a short drive from the Palomar campground, with a small museum on the 200-inch Hale Telescope
  • Fish Doane Pond at Palomar, stocked and easy for a first try
  • Detour to the Kwaay Paay and Garnet Peak overlooks at Laguna for sunset views east into the desert
  • Walk or drive into Julian from William Heise for pie, cider, and small-town shops
  • Book one of Heise's camping cabins if anyone in your group would rather skip the tent
  • Soak in the warm mineral pools at Agua Caliente County Park, a short drive from either desert campground (it also has its own cabins and yurts)
  • Drive Borrego Springs' back roads to see the metal sculptures scattered across Galleta Meadows
  • Time a spring visit for the wildflower bloom, which can carpet the desert floor after a wet winter
  • Stargaze from any of the campgrounds; Anza-Borrego is an International Dark Sky Park, and the mountain sites sit well above the coastal light and haze

Know before you go

This region spans about 600 to 6,000 feet, so the mountains and the desert are really two different trips. Laguna, Palomar, and William Heise all see occasional winter snow and can drop well below freezing at night, even when it's warm and sunny in the city an hour away. Anza-Borrego runs the opposite direction: summer highs regularly top 100°F, so October through April is the season to actually camp there, not just visit for the day.

An Adventure Pass is required to park at most trailheads and day-use areas in the Cleveland National Forest, including much of Laguna Mountain; it isn't needed at the developed campgrounds themselves, and Anza-Borrego uses its own park entrance and camping fees instead. Rattlesnakes are active in the backcountry areas from spring through fall, and flash floods are a real risk in desert washes and canyons after storms, even ones that look distant. If you want the cabins at William Heise, or the cabins and yurts at Agua Caliente, reserve as early as the system allows; they go faster than the tent sites.
