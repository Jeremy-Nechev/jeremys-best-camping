Laguna, Cuyamaca & Palomar Mountains

San Diego Mountains

Oak-shaded mountains all within about two hours of the city, with a cabin option if you'd rather skip the tent.

Where to camp

Mountain campgrounds under oak and pine.

Laguna Campground

Mt Laguna campground is set in a pine forest overlooking a meadow. There are many lakes that you can hike to. The best campsites are in the Meadow Loop, because they overlook the meadow. The most spacious campsites are in the Shady and Sunny loops. This campground is a lot better than its neighbor, Burnt Rancheria, because it is in a more scenic area and there is no road noise.

6,000 ft
Sites
104
Season
Year-round

6 mo out
  • Cold at night even in summer
  • Big Laguna Trail starts nearby
  • Adventure Pass needed for day parking

William Heise County Park

William Heise Campground is in William Heise County Park, only ten minutes from Julian. This campground has it all: spacious campsites, showers, playgrounds, cabin rentals with electricity (about $75) and even 5 miles of trails.

Elevation
4,200 ft
Sites
103
Season
Year-round
Reserve
6 mo out
  • Camping cabins sleep up to five and come with beds, a table, and a covered porch; bring your own bedding and cook outside
  • Cabins book out faster than the tent sites, especially in fall
  • Short drive down to Julian for pie and supplies

Things to do

This region covers more ground than its name suggests: high pine country at Laguna, granite peaks and a lake at Cuyamaca, sequoia-scale conifers at Palomar, and oak woodland around Julian, all within about two hours of the city.

  • Tour the Palomar Observatory, a short drive from the Palomar campground, with a small museum on the 200-inch Hale Telescope
  • Fish Doane Pond at Palomar, stocked and easy for a first try
  • Rent a rowboat or fish for trout and bass at Lake Cuyamaca, a short drive from either Cuyamaca campground
  • Detour to the Kwaaymii Point and Garnet Peak overlooks at Laguna for sunset views east into the desert
  • Go to Julian from William Heise or Cuyamaca for apple pie, cider, and small-town shops
  • Go to Eagle Peak Mine, an actual historic gold mine
  • Stargaze from any of the campgrounds; the mountain sites sit well above the coastal light and haze

Know before you go

This region spans about 4,000 to 6,000 feet. Laguna, Cuyamaca, Palomar, and William Heise all see occasional winter snow and can drop well below freezing at night, even when it's warm and sunny in the city an hour away.

An Adventure Pass or Reservation.gov digital version is required to park at most trailheads and day-use areas in the Cleveland National Forest, including much of Laguna Mountain; it isn't needed at the developed campgrounds themselves, including Cuyamaca Rancho State Park. Rattlesnakes are active in the backcountry areas from spring through fall. If you want the cabins at William Heise, reserve as early as the system allows because they book up faster than the tent sites.
