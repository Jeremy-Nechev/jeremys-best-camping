Where to camp

Three desert options in and around Anza-Borrego Desert State Park if you want warm winter sun instead.

Things to do

Open desert down from the San Diego mountains, all within about two hours of the city.

  • Soak in the warm mineral pools at Agua Caliente County Park, a short drive from either desert campground (it also has its own cabins and yurts)
  • Drive Borrego Springs' back roads to see the metal sculptures scattered across Galleta Meadows
  • Time a spring visit for the wildflower bloom, which can carpet the desert floor after a wet winter
  • Stargaze from either campground; Anza-Borrego is an International Dark Sky Park

Know before you go

This region spans about 600 to 1,400 feet, so it's a different trip from the cooler mountains up the road. Summer highs regularly top 100°F, so October through April is the season to actually camp here, not just visit for the day.

Anza-Borrego uses its own park entrance and camping fees. Rattlesnakes are active in the backcountry from spring through fall, and flash floods are a real risk in desert washes and canyons after storms, even ones that look distant. If you want the cabins at Agua Caliente, reserve as early as the system allows because they book up faster than the tent sites.
