Desert

Anza-Borrego

Open desert down from the San Diego mountains, warm winter sun when the mountains are cold.

Where to camp

Two desert options in Anza-Borrego Desert State Park if you want warm winter sun instead.

Palm Canyon Campground

Anza-Borrego Desert State Park's main campground, right at Borrego Springs, next to the park visitor center.

Elevation
600 ft
Sites
120
Season
Oct–Apr
Reserve
6 mo out
  • Trailhead to the Borrego Palm Canyon oasis starts here
  • Flush toilets and showers, the most developed desert option
  • Book well ahead for the March wildflower bloom

Tamarisk Grove Campground

A smaller, quieter campground at the junction of Highway 78 and County Route S3, central to the park's back roads.

Elevation
1,400 ft
Sites
27
Season
Oct–Apr
Reserve
6 mo out
  • Narrows Earth Trail and Yaqui Well Nature Trail both start nearby
  • Good base for exploring Blair Valley and Box Canyon
  • Smaller and shadier than Palm Canyon, with fewer facilities

Hikes

A couple of short desert trails, one flat and easy, one a real hike to a palm oasis.

Narrows Earth Trail

A short, flat interpretive loop near Tamarisk Grove explaining the geology of the surrounding badlands.

Distance
0.6 mi loop
Gain
Flat
Difficulty
Easy
Type
Loop
  • Numbered posts match a self-guided brochure
  • Little shade, so go in the morning
  • Good short stop right off Highway 78

Borrego Palm Canyon Trail

Starting at of Palm Canyon Campground, this hike goes up a rocky wash to a grove of native California fan palms and a seasonal waterfall.

Distance
3.0 mi RT
Gain
450 ft
Difficulty
Moderate
Type
Out-and-back
  • The railhead is a short walk from the campground
  • Bighorn sheep are sometimes seen on the canyon walls
  • Best in cool months; this one is brutal in summer heat

Things to do

Open desert down from the San Diego mountains, all within about two hours of the city.

  • Soak in the warm mineral pools at Agua Caliente County Park, a short drive from either desert campground (it also has its own cabins and yurts)
  • Drive Borrego Springs' back roads to see the metal sculptures scattered across Galleta Meadows
  • Time a spring visit for the wildflower bloom, which can carpet the desert floor after a wet winter
  • Stargaze from either campground; Anza-Borrego is an International Dark Sky Park

Know before you go

This region spans about 600 to 1,400 feet, so it's a different trip from the cooler mountains up the road. Summer highs regularly top 100°F, so October through April is the season to actually camp here, not just visit for the day.

Anza-Borrego uses its own park entrance and camping fees. Rattlesnakes are active in the backcountry from spring through fall, and flash floods are a real risk in desert washes and canyons after storms, even ones that look distant. If you want the cabins at Agua Caliente, reserve as early as the system allows because they book up faster than the tent sites.
