San Bernardino Mountains

Big Bear Area

Pine forest at 6,750 feet, close enough to leave after work on Friday.

Where to camp

Serrano

The developed option: flush toilets, showers, and lakefront access. Book early for summer weekends.

6,800 ft
Sites
132
Season
May–Oct
Reserve
6 mo out

  • Showers are coin-operated; bring quarters
  • Bear boxes at every site

Hanna Flat

Quieter and more forested, up in the Big Bear backcountry. First-come sites go fast on holiday weekends.

Elevation
7,000 ft
Sites
88
Season
May–Sep
Reserve
Partial
  • Vault toilets only, no showers
  • Nearest water is at the host site
  • Cell service is spotty

Pineknot

Walking distance to the village trailheads. Good base if you want to hike and eat in town.

Elevation
7,000 ft
Sites
48
Season
May–Oct
Reserve
6 mo out
  • Steep access road; watch trailer clearance
  • Shaded most of the day
  • Close to Grand View Point trail

Barton Flats / San Gorgonio

Not technically Big Bear, but only about 40 minutes down the mountain on Highway 38, these two neighboring campgrounds are a solid overflow option when Big Bear's lakeside sites are booked up or overcrowded.

Elevation
6,300 ft
Sites
90+
Season
May–Oct
Reserve
6 mo out
  • The two campgrounds sit next to each other along Highway 38
  • Quieter and cheaper than most Big Bear-proper sites
  • Jenks Lake is a short drive away and worth the stop

A few short, flat options for families with little ones, plus the classics if you're up for more.

Things to do

  • Rent a kayak or pontoon boat at one of the lake's marinas
  • Fish for the trout and bass the lake is stocked with
  • Ride the alpine coaster or zip line at the south shore resorts in summer
  • Pick up a Junior Ranger booklet or join a nature program at the Big Bear Discovery Center
  • Browse the village's restaurants and shops for an evening off from camp cooking
  • Ski or ride the gondola for scenic views come winter; some mountain-bike trail access runs even outside ski season
  • Stay at Pineknot Campground if you want to walk to the village trailheads and back for dinner without moving the car
  • Picnic or fish at Jenks Lake, a small no-motors lake near Barton Flats and San Gorgonio campgrounds

Know before you go

Big Bear sits at 6,750 feet, so afternoons are pleasant even in summer but nights can dip near freezing; pack warm layers regardless of season. Snow is common from December through March, and tire chains are often required on the mountain highways, even for four-wheel-drive vehicles.

Bear activity is real here, not just a trailhead name; use the provided bear boxes and never leave food or trash out overnight. Summer weekends and holidays get crowded on the lake and in the village, so arrive early if you want parking near the popular spots. A National Forest Adventure Pass is required to park at many trailheads outside the developed campgrounds.
