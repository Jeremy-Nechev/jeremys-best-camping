Things to do

  • Take a canoe tour with the Mono Lake Committee to get out among the tufa towers on the water
  • Ride the June Mountain gondola in summer for lake and Sierra crest views
  • Stop at the Mono Basin Scenic Area Visitor Center for the geology and history of the lake
  • Drive the June Lake Loop for lake views without the Mammoth traffic
  • Watch the swallows and gulls that nest on the lake's islands in early summer

Know before you go

Mono Lake sits around 6,400 feet, noticeably lower and warmer than the Mammoth Lakes basin, which makes it a good option when the high country is still under snow in late spring or has turned cold by early fall. June Lake and its campgrounds run closer to 7,600 feet and generally open a little later. The June Lake Loop road is open year-round, but side roads like the one to Parker Lake and Lundy Canyon can hold snow or be washed out early season, so check conditions before you drive up.

Mono Lake is a closed basin three times saltier than the ocean, so swimming is more of a novelty float than a swim, and there's no fishing in the lake itself, though June Lake and the nearby creeks are stocked. Wind can pick up on the lake by afternoon, which matters if you're canoeing or kayaking. As at Mammoth, cell service is unreliable away from the main highway, and bear boxes are standard at every campground on the loop.
