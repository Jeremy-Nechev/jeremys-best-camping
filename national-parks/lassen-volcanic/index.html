A few short, flat options for families with little ones, plus the classics if you're up for more.

Things to do

  • Rent a kayak or canoe at Manzanita Lake for a calm paddle with reflection views of the peak
  • Fish Manzanita or Summit Lake
  • Drive Highway 89 through the hydrothermal areas, past Sulphur Works, up to the Lassen Peak trailhead
  • Visit the Loomis Museum near Manzanita Lake for a kid-friendly exhibit on the park's 1915 eruption
  • Catch an evening ranger program at Manzanita Lake; check the visitor center for the schedule
  • Pick up a Junior Ranger booklet at the Loomis Museum or Kohm Yah-mah-nee Visitor Center

Know before you go

Entry is $30 per vehicle for seven days. The main park road, Highway 89, usually opens fully by late June or July and closes with the first heavy snow, often by late October; check current conditions before you plan a shoulder-season trip. Elevations run from 5,900 feet at Manzanita Lake to over 10,000 feet at the summit, so expect cold nights even in summer.

Bear boxes are required at every campground and rangers do enforce it. Cell service is limited to nonexistent through most of the park, and there's none at all on the Butte Lake side. Hydrothermal areas like Bumpass Hell are genuinely dangerous off the boardwalk; the ground can be a thin crust over scalding water.
