Things to do

  • Drive the Newton B. Drury Scenic Parkway, an old highway alignment through unbroken old growth
  • Watch for Roosevelt elk in the meadow at Elk Prairie, especially at dawn and dusk
  • Walk the tide pools at Enderts Beach near Crescent City at low tide
  • Stop at the Kuchel Visitor Center for a Junior Ranger booklet and tide charts
  • Drive out to the coast at Gold Bluffs Beach for sunset, with the redwoods at your back

Know before you go

Redwood is actually a joint operation between the National Park Service and California State Parks, and there's no entrance fee. Campgrounds run near sea level and stay mild year-round, but fog rolls in most mornings and afternoons, even in summer, so pack layers regardless of the season. The park stretches nearly 40 miles north to south along Highway 101, and driving between areas takes longer than the map suggests.

Cell service is unreliable to nonexistent through most of the park; download maps and directions before you go. Some side roads, including the one to Fern Canyon, are unpaved and close after heavy rain, so check conditions at a visitor center first. Roosevelt elk look docile but are not; keep well back, especially during the fall rut and spring calving season.
