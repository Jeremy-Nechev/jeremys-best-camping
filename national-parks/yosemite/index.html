Where to camp

Pines Campgrounds (Lower, Upper & North Pines)

The three Valley campgrounds, all pretty much the same: a great location, but very small campsites. Reservations are extremely difficult.

Elevation
4,000 ft
Sites
480+ combined
Season
Most of year
Reserve
5 mo out
  • Reservations open on the 15th, five months ahead, and sites go in under a minute
  • Upper Pines stays open year-round; Lower and North Pines close for part of the winter
  • Shuttle stop inside or near every loop

Bridalveil Creek

There may be no better view in the world than the one from glacier point. From Bridalveil creek, it is only 15 minutes away! It is also only 20 minutes to Yosemite Valley. Most campsites are small, but there are a few big ones.

Summerdale

Outside the park

Summerdale campground is located in a scenic, wildflower filled meadow, only 2 miles to the south entrance to yosemite. Some of the campsites are set along Big Creek. It is five minutes to the Mariposa Grove of Giant Sequoias. Unfortunately, it is over an hour to Yosemite Valley. But this campground is definitely the most scenic in the Yosemite area.

Elevation
5,000 ft
Sites
30
Season
May–Sep
Reserve
6 mo out
  • Good base for the Mariposa Grove, less so for the Valley
  • Managed by the Forest Service, not the park

The Primitive Four

These are four primitive campgrounds to use if the others are full or if you want that experience. Yosemite Creek is very remote, but the sites are big and there is a good chance of scoring one. Porcupine flat is just off the highway, and can feel crowded. White Wolf is the best of the four, and has a good location. Tamarack Flat is another good option.

Elevation
6,300–8,100 ft
Sites
~90 combined
Season
Jun–Sep
Reserve
First-come
  • No water at most of these; pack it in
  • White Wolf is the best pick if you have a choice
  • Good backups when the Valley and Tuolumne are full

Dimond "O"

Outside the park

Dimond “O” is a small, quiet and pretty campground just 15 minutes outside the Big Oak Flat entrance to Yosemite. This campground has very large and private sites, some of which are riverfront. Swimming in the river is a popular activity in the summertime. Note: Over an hour to the valley.

Things to do

Half Dome is a very cool activity(14 to 16 miles round trip with cables at the top, and a permit that goes into a lottery months ahead), but there's plenty to fill a day without it.

  • Rent a bike at Yosemite Lodge and ride the flat path past El Capitan Meadow
  • Walk to the base of Yosemite Falls, which runs hardest in May and can be dry by August
  • Drive Tioga Road once it opens for granite domes with no crowds
  • Catch a ranger talk at the Valley Visitor Center or Glacier Point in the evening
  • Stargaze from Glacier Point after the day-trippers leave
  • Pick up a free Junior Ranger booklet at the Valley Visitor Center
  • Cool off at the riverside beaches near the Swinging Bridge in the valley
  • Ride the flat bike path loop around the Valley floor

Know before you go

Entry costs $35 per vehicle, good for seven days, or is covered by an America the Beautiful annual pass. A campsite reservation does not include park entry. Cell service is unreliable through most of the park; download offline maps before you arrive.

Don't leave food out or even in your car because of bears. Use bear lockers for anything scented. Elevations range from 4,000 feet in the Valley to over 8,600 feet at Tuolumne Meadows, so pack layers even in summer. Tioga Road, the only route into Tuolumne Meadows, is closed by snow between from November through late May or June, depending on the year.
