A few short, flat options for families with little ones, plus the classics if you're up for more.

Things to do

Half Dome is the one everyone asks about (14 to 16 miles round trip with cables at the top, and a permit that goes into a lottery months ahead), but there's plenty to fill a day without it.

  • Rent a bike at Yosemite Lodge and ride the flat path past El Capitan Meadow
  • Walk to the base of Yosemite Falls, which runs hardest in May and can be dry by August
  • Drive Tioga Road once it opens for granite domes with no crowds
  • Catch a ranger talk at the Valley Visitor Center or Glacier Point in the evening
  • Stargaze from Glacier Point after the day-trippers leave
  • Pick up a free Junior Ranger booklet at the Valley Visitor Center
  • Cool off at the outdoor pool in Curry Village
  • Ride the flat bike path loop around the Valley floor, good for kids on training wheels

Know before you go

Entry costs $35 per vehicle, good for seven days, or is covered by an America the Beautiful annual pass. A campsite reservation does not include park entry. Cell service is unreliable through most of the park; download offline maps before you arrive.

This is bear country. Every site has a metal food locker, and rangers do check. Never leave food, coolers, or scented items in your car overnight. Elevations range from 4,000 feet in the Valley to over 8,600 feet at Tuolumne Meadows, so pack layers even in summer, and give yourself a day to adjust if you're headed straight to the high country. Tioga Road, the only route into Tuolumne Meadows, is closed by snow roughly from November through late May or June, depending on the year.
