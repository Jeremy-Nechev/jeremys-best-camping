California

Sequoia & Kings Canyon

The largest trees on earth, and a canyon most visitors never drive into.

Where to camp

The three park campgrounds below anchor their own small clusters of sites, plus a few quieter National Forest options and a lake with an actual beach.

Lodgepole

Central to the giant forest, with a market and the shortest walk to the big trees.

6,700 ft
Sites
214
Season
Apr–Nov
Reserve
6 mo out
  • Shuttle to General Sherman in summer
  • Bear activity is high, so use the boxes
  • Market and showers nearby

Sunset

Grant Grove side, big and forested, with easy access to the sequoias. One of three campgrounds clustered together here, alongside Azalea and Crystal Springs.

Elevation
6,500 ft
Sites
157
Season
May–Sep
Reserve
6 mo out
  • Walk to Grant Grove village
  • Sunset views over the foothills
  • Azalea and Crystal Springs are a short walk up the road if Sunset is full; same village, same amenities

Moraine

Down in Kings Canyon along the river. Hot in summer, and worth the drive down. Part of the same short cluster as Sentinel and Sheep Creek in Cedar Grove.

4,600 ft
Sites
121
Season
May–Sep
Reserve
6 mo out
  • Highway 180 closes in winter
  • Riverside sites are the good ones
  • Sentinel and Sheep Creek are just up the road, same Cedar Grove cluster, worth checking if Moraine is full

Upper Stony Creek

A small, quiet National Forest campground on Generals Highway, between the two parks and well off the main crowds.

Elevation
6,400 ft
Sites
18

May–Sep
Reserve
6 mo out
  • Run by the Forest Service, not the park, so it's a good overflow when park campgrounds are booked
  • Near Stony Creek Village for a quick supply run
  • Trailhead access into the Jennie Lakes Wilderness

Princess

Another quieter National Forest option, on Highway 180 near the Hume Lake turnoff.

5,900 ft
Sites

May–Sep
Reserve

  • Close to Hume Lake for a swim after setting up camp
  • Some sites are first-come, so there's a fallback even if reservations are gone

A reservoir with an actual sand beach, on the National Forest side of the boundary between the two parks.

5,200 ft

  • Small boats and paddleboards allowed; no gas motors
  • Store and snack bar nearby in summer

Hikes

A few short, flat options for families with little ones, plus the classics if you're up for more.

A flat, paved boardwalk loop around a meadow at the Giant Forest Museum, with sequoias the whole way around.

Things to do

  • See General Sherman, the largest tree on earth by volume; go early or late to skip the tour bus crowds
  • Tour Crystal Cave, a marble cavern in the foothills (advance ticket only, sold at the Foothills or Lodgepole visitor center, not the cave itself; a narrow, chilly walk not ideal for very young kids)
  • Drive Highway 180 down into Kings Canyon, one of the most dramatic road trips in the Sierra
  • Stop at Grizzly Falls along the Kings Canyon road
  • Wade the Kings River on the canyon floor on a hot day
  • Swim the sand beach at Hume Lake, the easiest real swimming spot in the area
  • Pick up a free Junior Ranger booklet at either visitor center
  • Stop by the Giant Forest Museum for hands-on exhibits, good for a break from walking

Know before you go

Entry is $35 per vehicle for seven days and covers both Sequoia and Kings Canyon. Generals Highway, connecting the two halves of the park, is narrow and winding with tight switchbacks; trailers and larger RVs should check current length restrictions before heading up. Tire chains are required in winter storms even for vehicles with four-wheel drive.

Elevations range from about 1,700 feet in the Kings Canyon foothills to over 6,700 feet at Lodgepole, so pack for both heat and cold depending on where you're headed. Bear activity is high throughout the park; use the food lockers at every site and never leave a cooler out. Cell service is unreliable to nonexistent almost everywhere in the park.
