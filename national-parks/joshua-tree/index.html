Things to do

  • Rock climb or boulder at Hidden Valley or Jumbo Rocks; outfitters in town run half-day intro lessons
  • Drive up to Keys View for sunset over the Coachella Valley and the San Andreas Fault
  • Plan at least one night around the dark sky here, among the best within driving distance of Los Angeles; new moon weekends are best
  • Walk the flat, shaded Oasis of Mara boardwalk at the park's north entrance
  • Loop the Cholla Cactus Garden to see the "jumping" chollas up close (hands to yourself)
  • Pick up a Junior Ranger booklet at either visitor center
  • Stock up on coffee and supplies in the town of Joshua Tree before you head in; there's no cell service or much water once you're past the entrance station

Know before you go

Entry is $30 per vehicle for seven days, separate from your campsite fee. There is no reliable water anywhere in the park except at Black Rock and Cottonwood, so fill up before you head to any other campground. Cell service is spotty to nonexistent past the entrance stations.

Summer daytime highs regularly top 100°F with almost no shade, which makes October through April the better window for camping and hiking here; winter nights can still drop below freezing at elevation. Flash floods are a real risk in washes and canyons after storms, even ones that look distant. Rattlesnakes and scorpions are part of the desert; watch where you put your hands and feet on the rocks.
