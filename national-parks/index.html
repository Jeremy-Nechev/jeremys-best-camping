Getting a reservation

All five parks book through recreation.gov, and all five run on the same basic rule: reservations open exactly five or six months before the arrival date, at 7 a.m. Pacific time, and the popular loops go in minutes. Create an account ahead of time, have a couple of campground and site numbers picked out in advance, and be logged in and ready a few minutes early.

If you miss the release, don't give up. Cancellations post throughout the season, and checking back a few times a week, especially a week or two before your dates, turns up real openings. First-come, first-served campgrounds and partial-reservation sites exist in every park too; they're a good backup plan or a good option if your trip is flexible enough to travel midweek.

Families should also budget extra time for the drive: mountain roads into these parks are winding, and posted travel times can run long with kids who need stops. Building in a buffer before your campsite check-in window avoids a stressful first evening.
