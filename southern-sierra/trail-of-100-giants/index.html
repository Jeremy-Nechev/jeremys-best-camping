Sequoia National Forest

Trail of 100 Giants & Ponderosa

A sequoia grove you can walk in twenty minutes, and a handful of small forest campgrounds strung along the highway south of it. About three hours from LA, and a fraction of the crowds you'd find at the national park entrances.

Where to camp

Redwood Meadow

Small and first-come, right at the Trail of 100 Giants trailhead. Sites sit under the sequoias themselves.

Sites

Reserve
First-come

  • Vault toilets, no showers
  • Fills by early afternoon on summer weekends

Holey Meadow

The smallest and least-known option, tucked just off the highway with meadow views. Easy to miss, worth the stop.

Hikes

One flat grove walk, and one climb to a lookout if you want to earn the view.

Trail of 100 Giants Loop

A paved and boardwalked loop through a dense grove of giant sequoias, some over 1,500 years old. No permit, no crowd control gate, just a pullout and a trailhead.

0.6 mi loop

Needles Lookout Trail

A climb from Quaking Aspen out to a historic fire lookout perched on a granite spire, with views down into the Kern River canyon.

  • Little shade, go early in summer

Things to do

  • Walk the Trail of 100 Giants at sunrise or sunset to have the grove to yourself
  • Grab a burger or restock supplies at the store and lodge in Ponderosa, at the top of the Western Divide Highway
  • Drive the full length of the Western Divide Highway, a scenic byway connecting Highway 190 to Highway 155
  • Fish Freeman Creek below Redwood Meadow
  • Stargaze from Holey Meadow; there's almost no light pollution up here

Know before you go

Ponderosa, at the north end of the Western Divide Highway, has the last gas, groceries, and sit-down food before you head south into the campgrounds. It's a good idea to top off there rather than count on cell service to find something later, coverage is spotty to nonexistent for most of this area.

The Western Divide Highway is paved but narrow and winding, with drop-offs in places; take it slow, especially towing a trailer. The road closes with the first real snow, typically sometime in November, and doesn't reopen until late spring.

Campfire and stove restrictions are common in late summer and vary by year, check with the Sequoia National Forest ranger station in Springville before you go. A few of these campgrounds don't have potable water some years, bring more than you think you need.
