Things to do

  • Rent a kayak or paddleboard at Kiva Beach or Sand Harbor and get out early, before the afternoon wind picks up
  • Swim the clear water at Sand Harbor on the Nevada side, or catch its Shakespeare festival most summers
  • Let younger kids swim the shallow, gentle water at Kiva or Baldwin Beach instead of the deeper drop-offs elsewhere on the lake
  • Pull off at the Emerald Bay overlook on Highway 89 for the best photo on the lake, even if you're not hiking Eagle Falls
  • Ski Palisades Tahoe or Heavenly in winter, both within an hour of most campgrounds (though most sites here close for the season before the snow arrives)

Know before you go

Most campgrounds on the lake run from late May or June through September or October and require a reservation well ahead of the season. Elevation here is around 6,200 to 6,400 feet, so afternoon sun feels stronger than it looks and nights stay cool even in midsummer. The lake itself rarely warms above the mid-60s, even in August.

Bear boxes are standard at every campground on the lake; black bears are common and comfortable around people, so never leave food or coolers out. Traffic around the lake, especially on Highway 89 near Emerald Bay, backs up badly on summer weekends, so plan drives for early morning. Wildfire smoke can affect air quality some summers; check conditions if you have anyone in your group with respiratory concerns.
